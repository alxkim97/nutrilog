📋 Projection Summary ▾
📚 Protein Intake — The 1.2–2.4 g/kg Debate Explained ▾

The wide range you see (1.2–2.4 g/kg) is real, and all values are cited in legitimate research. Here's why they differ:

1.2–1.4 g/kg — The minimum to avoid muscle breakdown in healthy sedentary adults. Used in older WHO/FAO reports. Not enough if you're training.
Source: WHO/FAO/UNU, Human Energy Requirements, 2004
1.6–1.8 g/kg — Meta-analysis sweet spot for muscle gain in training individuals (not cutting). Morton et al. 2018 (Br J Sports Med) analysed 49 studies — muscle gains plateau around 1.62 g/kg/day.
Source: Morton RW et al. Br J Sports Med. 2018;52:376–384.
⭐ 2.4 g/kg — Your current target (high-protein recomp + cholesterol-aware fat)
When you're in a caloric deficit, muscle breakdown risk rises. High protein counteracts this and supports new synthesis simultaneously. 2.4 g/kg is at the upper-effective range — safe and proven to maximise muscle retention during a cut.
Sources: Helms et al. JISSN 2014;11:20 · Longland et al. AJCN 2016;103(3):738 · Schoenfeld & Aragon JISSN 2018;15:10
Fat at 20% of calories — Adjusted from standard 27% to support your cholesterol management. Essential fats still covered; prioritise unsaturated sources (fish, avocado, olive oil).
Note: Monitor cholesterol with your doctor; dietary fat type matters as much as quantity.
