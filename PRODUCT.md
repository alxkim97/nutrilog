# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Alex Kim — solo user, daily active. Uses the app at home on a desktop, typically morning and evening, logging meals and checking progress. Comes to it with a clear intent: log food fast, check numbers, move on. Not a casual user; knows exactly what they want to see.

## Product Purpose

NutriLog is a personal macro nutrition tracker for body recomposition. It logs daily meals against protein/fat/carbs/fiber targets, tracks history and streaks, syncs to cloud, and projects body composition over time. Success looks like: open the app, log a meal in under 10 seconds, close the app knowing where you stand.

## Brand Personality

Clean · Calm · Motivated

Tone: confident and quiet. The app trusts the user to know what they're doing. No motivational copy, no celebration spam — just clear data presented with care. When something is good, it shows; it doesn't announce it.

## Anti-references

- Fitness influencer apps: loud gradients, neon accents, pump-up energy, "LET'S GO" copy, rainbow macros. Aggressive and impersonal.
- Generic SaaS dashboards: blue accent, sidebar + cards, could be any B2B tool, no personality.
- MyFitnessPal: cluttered, ad-heavy, corporate health-app green.

## Design Principles

1. **Speed over decoration.** Every interaction should feel fast. Animations exist to orient, not to impress.
2. **Numbers are the hero.** Macro values, calorie counts, and streak days are the content. Typography and layout serve them — not the other way around.
3. **Quiet confidence.** The app knows what it's doing. No tooltips explaining the obvious, no empty-state illustrations, no progress bars on things that don't need them.
4. **Raycast density.** Information-dense but never cluttered. Every element has a reason to exist at that size, in that position.
5. **Personal, not clinical.** This is one person's data. It can feel owned and familiar without being warm or soft.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Personal use only — no formal WCAG requirement. Maintain readable contrast and sensible focus states as good engineering practice.
