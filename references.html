01
Body Mass Index (BMI)
A simple screening tool based on height and weight. Used in NutriLog to give a quick health context — not used to calculate calorie targets.
Formula
BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²
Example: 77 kg ÷ (1.91 m)² = 21.1 kg/m²
BMI RangeClassificationStatus
< 18.5Underweight⚠ Monitor
18.5 – 24.9Normal / Healthy weight✓ Ideal
25.0 – 29.9Overweight⚠ Elevated risk
30.0 – 34.9Obese Class I✗ High risk
35.0 – 39.9Obese Class II✗ Very high risk
≥ 40.0Obese Class III (Severe)✗ Extreme risk
⚠️
BMI limitations: BMI does not distinguish between fat mass and muscle mass. A muscular athlete can show "overweight" BMI while being lean. Use it alongside body fat % for a more complete picture. BMI is less reliable for individuals under 18, over 65, or of Asian descent (where risk thresholds are lower).
🔵
In NutriLog: BMI is displayed in Settings → Personal Info and Projection page as an informational metric. It does not influence calorie or macro targets.

Source: World Health Organization. BMI classification. WHO, 2000. [1]

02
Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)
The number of calories your body burns at complete rest — just to maintain basic organ function. NutriLog uses two different formulas for two different purposes.
🔵
NutriLog uses two formulas: Harris-Benedict Revised for the Settings page (TDEE & calorie target), and Mifflin-St Jeor for the weekly-recalculated Projection model.
Harris-Benedict Revised — Settings Page
♂ Male: 88.362 + (13.397 × weight_kg) + (4.799 × height_cm) − (5.677 × age_years) ♀ Female: 447.593 + (9.247 × weight_kg) + (3.098 × height_cm) − (4.330 × age_years)
Mifflin-St Jeor — Projection Model (weekly recalc)
♂ Male: (10 × weight_kg) + (6.25 × height_cm) − (5 × age_years) + 5 ♀ Female: (10 × weight_kg) + (6.25 × height_cm) − (5 × age_years) − 161
📊
Which is more accurate? Mifflin-St Jeor (1990) is generally considered more accurate for modern populations — studies show it predicts RMR within 10% for most people. Harris-Benedict Revised (Roza & Shizgal, 1984) tends to slightly overestimate BMR in obese individuals. Neither accounts for muscle mass directly — use body fat % for more precision.

Sources: Roza AM, Shizgal HM. Am J Clin Nutr. 1984;40(1):168–182. [2] · Mifflin MD et al. Am J Clin Nutr. 1990;51(2):241–247. [3]

03
TDEE & Activity Multipliers
Total Daily Energy Expenditure — how many calories you actually burn per day, accounting for physical activity.
Formula
TDEE = BMR × Activity Multiplier
Example: BMR 1,871 × 1.55 (4–5× / week) = 2,900 kcal/day
Activity LevelDescriptionMultiplierUsed in NutriLog
SedentaryDesk job, no exercise× 1.200No exercise
Lightly ActiveLight exercise 1–3 days/week× 1.3751–3× / week
Moderately ActiveModerate exercise 4–5 days/week× 1.5504–5× / week ⭐ default
Very ActiveHard training 6–7 days/week× 1.7256–7× / week
Extra ActivePhysical job or 2× daily training× 1.9002× / day
⚠️
Most people overestimate their activity level. Research shows self-reported activity is typically 20–30% higher than measured. If weight loss stalls, try dropping one level (e.g., "4–5×" → "1–3×") before reducing calories further.

Source: Harris JA, Benedict FG. A Biometric Study of Human Basal Metabolism. Proc Natl Acad Sci. 1918. [4]

04
Lean Body Mass (LBM)
Everything in your body that isn't fat — muscle, bone, organs, water, and connective tissue. Used to calculate protein targets and the fat-to-lose estimate.
Formula
LBM = weight (kg) × (1 − body fat % ÷ 100)
Example: 77 kg × (1 − 16 ÷ 100) = 64.7 kg LBM
🔵
In NutriLog: LBM is used as the basis for protein target calculation (LBM × 2.4 g/kg) and to set a floor on the Goal Weight — you cannot set a goal weight below your current LBM, as that would imply losing muscle.
📊
Fat to Lose formula: Fat to Lose = max(0, weight − max(goal_weight, LBM))
This ensures the calculated fat to lose is always ≥ 0, and goal weight is always ≥ LBM. Example: 77 kg − max(72 kg, 64.7 kg) = 77 − 72 = 5 kg fat to lose.

How NutriLog calculates your daily calorie target for each goal type.

Calorie Target = TDEE + Goal Adjustment

Maintenance± 0 kcal/day

Recomposition Auto-Deficit Formula
Deficit = (fat to lose (kg) × 7,700) ÷ (weeks × 7)
Clamped: min 50 kcal/day (never zero) · max 700 kcal/day (safety limit)
Example: 5 kg fat × 7,700 ÷ (20 weeks × 7 days) = 275 kcal/day deficit
⚠️
NutriLog flags timelines as aggressive when the required deficit exceeds 600 kcal/day. A deficit above 500–700 kcal/day significantly increases muscle loss risk, especially without high protein intake and resistance training.
07
The 7,700 kcal/kg Rule & Its Limitations
The widely used conversion between calorie deficit and fat loss — and why it overestimates results over time.
The Rule
1 kg body fat ≈ 7,700 kcal (≈ 3,500 kcal per pound)
Fat tissue is ~87% triglycerides (9 kcal/g) + water & connective tissue. Pure triglyceride = 9.44 kcal/g, but mixed adipose tissue averages ~7,700 kcal/kg.
⚠️
Why it overestimates after 4+ weeks: As you lose fat, your BMR drops (less body mass to maintain). Your body also becomes more metabolically efficient under sustained deficit — a process called metabolic adaptation. The 7,700 rule assumes a static metabolism, which overestimates fat loss by approximately 2× beyond 4 weeks.
🔵
NutriLog's Projection Model accounts for this by recalculating BMR weekly using your actual (decreasing) weight, and applying an adaptive factor of 0.95× from week 5 onward to simulate metabolic adaptation. See Section 13 for full model details.

Source: Hall KD et al. Lancet. 2011;378(9793):826–837. [6]

08
Protein Targets
The most debated macro in nutrition science. Here is what the evidence actually says at each intake level.
NutriLog Default Protein Target
Protein = body weight (kg) × 2.4 g
Example: 77 kg × 2.4 = 185 g/day
Minimum (floor): body weight × 1.8 g → 139 g/day
1.2 – 1.4 g/kg
Minimum to prevent muscle loss in sedentary healthy adults. WHO/FAO recommendation. Insufficient for active people or recomposition.
Minimum
1.6 – 1.8 g/kg
Meta-analysis sweet spot for muscle gain in resistance-trained individuals not in a deficit. Morton et al. (2018): gains plateau at ~1.62 g/kg across 49 studies.
Effective
2.0 – 2.4 g/kg
Optimal for body recomposition (simultaneous fat loss + muscle gain). Helms et al. (2014): 2.3–3.1 g/kg LBM for natural athletes in a deficit. NutriLog default.
⭐ NutriLog Default
2.4 – 3.1 g/kg
High-end recomposition range. Longland et al. (2016): 2.4 g/kg group gained more LBM than 1.2 g/kg group in a 40% deficit over 4 weeks. Safe but diminishing returns.
High-end safe
> 3.1 g/kg
No additional muscle benefit shown. Not harmful for healthy kidneys. Only useful if calorie budget is very restricted (protein is filling and thermogenic).
Diminishing returns
💡
Why higher protein during a deficit? In a caloric deficit, the body can break down muscle for energy. Elevated protein intake (≥ 2.0 g/kg) signals the body to preserve muscle and prioritise fat for fuel. This is especially important when training hard.
🔴
Kidney safety note: High protein intake (up to 2.4 g/kg) is safe for people with healthy kidneys. If you have pre-existing kidney disease, consult a nephrologist before exceeding 0.8 g/kg. NutriLog does not screen for kidney conditions.

Sources: Morton RW et al. Br J Sports Med. 2018. [7] · Helms ER et al. JISSN. 2014;11:20. [8] · Longland TM et al. Am J Clin Nutr. 2016;103(3):738–746. [9] · Stokes T et al. (ISSN Position Stand). JISSN. 2018;15:10. [10]

Dietary Fat Targets
Fat is essential for hormone production, fat-soluble vitamin absorption (A, D, E, K), and cell membrane integrity. Going too low is dangerous.
NutriLog Default Fat Target (standard)
Fat = (calorie target × 0.30) ÷ 9
30% of calories from fat · 9 kcal per gram of fat
Example: 2,517 kcal × 0.30 ÷ 9 = 84 g/day
Range: 20% floor (min) · 35% ceiling (max) per AMDR
Fat Intake% of Total CaloriesGuidance
< 20% of calories< 0.44 g/kcal✗ Too low — hormonal disruption risk
20 – 25% of calories0.44 – 0.56 g/kcalMinimum range (deficit phases)
25 – 35% of calories0.56 – 0.78 g/kcal✓ AMDR recommended range
> 35% of calories> 0.78 g/kcal⚠ Limits carbs/protein budget
💛
Cholesterol management mode: NutriLog has an optional cholesterol management setting that keeps fat within a medically conservative range (AHA: <7% saturated fat of total calories, <300mg dietary cholesterol/day). When enabled, fat targets are user-defined rather than auto-calculated.

Sources: Institute of Medicine. Dietary Reference Intakes for Energy, Carbohydrate, Fiber, Fat. 2005. [11] · American Heart Association. Dietary Fat and Cardiovascular Disease. Circulation. 2017. [12]

10
Carbohydrate Targets
Carbs are calculated last — from whatever calories remain after protein and fat are accounted for. This is the standard "protein-first" macro hierarchy used by most sports nutrition frameworks.
Formula — Residual Method
Carb kcal = Total kcal − (protein (g) × 4) − (fat (g) × 9)
Carbs (g) = Carb kcal ÷ 4
Example: 2,517 − (185 × 4) − (84 × 9) = 2,517 − 740 − 756 = 1,021 kcal → 255 g carbs
Protein: 4 kcal/g · Fat: 9 kcal/g · Carbohydrate: 4 kcal/g · Fiber: ~2 kcal/g (partially fermented)
🔵
Minimum carbohydrate intake: The brain requires ~130 g/day of glucose as a minimum (IOM). However, ketosis provides an alternative fuel source, so this minimum isn't absolute. NutriLog does not currently support ketogenic macro profiles.

Source: Institute of Medicine. Dietary Reference Intakes. 2005. [11]

11
Fiber Targets
Dietary fiber supports gut microbiome health, satiety, blood sugar control, and cardiovascular health. Most people eat less than half the recommended amount.
NutriLog Formula — IOM Adequate Intake
Fiber (g) = 14 × (calorie target ÷ 1,000)
Example: 14 × (2,517 ÷ 1,000) = 35 g/day
Hard cap: 60 g/day (GI discomfort at very high intakes) · Floor: 20 g/day minimum
Tolerance range applied: −20% to +25% of target
PopulationDaily Fiber TargetSource
Adult men (19–50)38 g/dayIOM Adequate Intake
Adult women (19–50)25 g/dayIOM Adequate Intake
Adults 51+Men: 30g · Women: 21gIOM (energy needs decrease)
NutriLog default (2,500 kcal)35 g/day14 g per 1,000 kcal
🌿
Why fiber matters for body composition: High-fiber foods are voluminous and low in calorie density, making them excellent for satiety during a deficit. Fiber also slows glucose absorption, reducing insulin spikes that promote fat storage. Fermentable fiber feeds beneficial gut bacteria that may influence body composition through hormonal pathways.

Source: Institute of Medicine. Dietary Reference Intakes for Energy, Carbohydrate, Fiber, Fat. 2005. [11]

Safe Weight Loss Rate
How fast you can lose weight while minimising muscle loss and metabolic adaptation.
Conservative (safe for most)
0.5% BW/week
At 77 kg → ~0.4 kg/week. Minimal muscle loss. Recommended for older adults, beginners, and those with a small amount to lose.
Moderate (active individuals)
0.5–1% BW/week
At 77 kg → 0.4–0.8 kg/week. Acceptable with high protein intake and resistance training. ISSN recommendation.
Aggressive (short-term only)
1–1.5% BW/week
At 77 kg → 0.8–1.2 kg/week. Significant muscle loss risk without very high protein. Not sustainable beyond 6–8 weeks.
NutriLog Safety Flag
≤ 700 kcal/day
NutriLog caps the auto-calculated deficit at 700 kcal/day and warns when the timeline would require more than 600 kcal/day.
👴👵
For adults over 60: Muscle loss with age (sarcopenia) is a serious concern. Aggressive deficits accelerate muscle loss significantly. The Gentle Loss (−200 kcal/day) goal in NutriLog is specifically designed for this population — ~0.2 kg/week fat loss with minimal muscle risk when paired with adequate protein.

Sources: Aragon AA et al. (ISSN Position Stand on Diets). JISSN. 2017;14:16. [13] · Helms ER et al. JISSN. 2014. [8]

13
Body Composition Projection Model
A week-by-week hybrid model that simulates fat loss and lean mass gain simultaneously. Unlike simple deficit calculators, it accounts for metabolic adaptation and weekly weight change.
1
Weekly BMR Recalculation (Mifflin-St Jeor)
Each week, BMR is recalculated using the model's current weight — not your starting weight. As you lose fat, BMR drops, making future fat loss slightly harder. This reflects real physiology.
BMR_week = (10 × weight) + (6.25 × height) − (5 × age) ± 5
2
Adaptive Factor (Week 5+)
From week 5 onward, the effective TDEE is multiplied by 0.95 to simulate metabolic adaptation — the body becoming more efficient under sustained deficit. This prevents the model from overestimating long-term fat loss.
TDEE_effective = BMR × activity × (week > 4 ? 0.95 : 1.0)
3
Weekly Fat Loss
Fat lost per week is calculated from the effective deficit, using 7,700 kcal/kg. A floor of TDEE − 1,200 kcal prevents the model from projecting starvation-level deficits.
fat_loss_kg = (min(deficit, max(50, TDEE − 1200)) × 7) ÷ 7700
4
Muscle Gain (Protein-Dependent)
Weekly lean mass gain is capped at 0.15 kg/week, scaled by how close protein intake is to 1.6 g/kg LBM (the minimum effective recomposition level). Gain tapers toward zero over ~60 weeks as the beginner advantage diminishes.
protein_adequacy = min(1, protein ÷ (LBM × 1.6)) muscle_gain = max(0, 0.15 × protein_adequacy × (1 − week / 60))
5
Fat Floor Safety
Fat mass is prevented from falling below 4% of body weight — the approximate essential fat minimum. This prevents the model from projecting biologically impossible outcomes.
fat = max(weight × 0.04, fat − fat_loss_kg)
⚠️
Model limitations: This is a deterministic simulation, not a personalised physiological model. Individual response varies significantly based on genetics, training programme, sleep quality, stress levels, and actual dietary adherence. Use the projection as a directional estimate, not a precise prediction. Weight fluctuations of ±1–2 kg from water retention are normal and not captured by the model.

Sources: Hall KD et al. Lancet. 2011. [6] · Schoenfeld BJ, Aragon AA. JISSN. 2018;15:10. [14]
